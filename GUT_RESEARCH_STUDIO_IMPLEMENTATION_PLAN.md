## 1. Decision: what to build

Build a **Gut Research Studio** around one object: a *living question dossier*. The dossier joins (a) what the person actually recorded, (b) competing explanations and missing facts, (c) what published research studied and how closely it fits, (d) one useful choice or clinician question, and (e) what changed afterward. Every connection has a source and a meaning. This is a research tool for understanding and preparing decisions, not a trigger detector, diagnostic device, treatment program or physiology simulator.

The memorable interaction should be: **“I thought this food explained my symptom. Show me the case for it, the case against it, and the one fact that would most improve my decision.”** On a sparse record, the honest result is “we cannot tell”; the product still offers a relevant source, a safe question, or a concise visit brief. It does not demand days of logging before it helps.

The intellectual invention is the *composition* of an inspectable personal argument graph, a research applicability check, a deliberate search for counterevidence, and a change-aware decision record. Similar components exist elsewhere; **do not market this as world first until a defensible prior-art review is complete**. Validate usefulness before claiming that it is worth $20/month.

## 4. The five connected product inventions

### A. Research Passport — turn a question into a researchable question

The person starts with the current four soothing textured Clinical cards: **I feel unwell / I need to choose / I want to understand / I have a care question**. They are genuinely clickable and lead into *different* concise prompts. A single question is enough. Behind the screen, a deterministic compiler creates a typed `GutResearchQuestion`: concern, candidate exposure or decision, comparison, outcome, time precision, known/unknown diagnosis, and desired decision. Ask at most one clarification if the answer would change the route. Preserve the person's exact words and show the structured interpretation for correction. No diagnostic label is inferred from symptoms.

**Why this matters:** published research is about populations, interventions and measured outcomes, while users ask “Why was I bloated after chai?” A passport is the bridge. The passport also prevents the app from searching and summarizing an unbounded personal narrative. Research queries use generic whitelisted terms; personal text stays private.

### B. Evidence Court — test an idea against its alternatives

For each user theory, create four expandable columns within one compact Clinical card: **supports**, **challenges**, **other possible context**, **unknown**. The first two accept only explicitly linked personal reports with stable source IDs. Other same-date meals, medication *notes*, sleep, stress or illness remain context, never votes for a cause. A separate general-research lane attaches to the *topic* rather than a personal event. Each edge explains why it exists, shows time precision and opens the exact source. The user can correct or exclude a mismatched meal variant. The system deliberately looks for counterexamples and asks, “What observation would weaken this idea?”

**Why this matters:** the user gets an actual research habit, not a prettier graph. Source revisions invalidate prior conclusions. A one-off “symptom after chai” is labeled one reported occasion, not a trigger. The Food Freedom branch foregrounds tolerated occasions and asks whether a proposed action would unnecessarily narrow the person's diet.

### C. Study-to-Me Lens — inspect whether a paper answers this question

Research results become claim cards with **population, condition, intervention/exposure, comparator, measured outcome, design, result, limitations, publication status, date, and exact source span**. Unknown fields say *unknown*. The user sees “This study examined adults diagnosed with IBS and measured global symptoms; your question is about one episode of reflux, so applicability is limited,” only when the metadata/full text actually supports those fields. Give each source an inspectable applicability checklist rather than a fake 0–100 score. Prefer reviewed guidelines and systematic reviews for broad background; primary studies remain visible with their limitations. Do not summarize an abstract as a treatment recommendation.

**Why this matters:** current Research Lens returns papers, but the user must do the hard interpretive work. This is the research layer that can change a decision without pretending to diagnose an individual.

### D. One-Fact Investigator — ask for the most decision-relevant missing fact

Select at most one optional question from the current dossier: symptom onset precision, the outcome of one stable meal occasion, whether two meal names refer to the same preparation, or whether a documented clinician instruction is current. Show *why this fact matters* and what answer paths it could change. Offer **I don't know**, **skip**, and **leave open**. Do not ask merely to raise a data-completeness score. If no answer would change a meaningful next step, ask nothing.

**Why this matters:** the person experiences an investigator, not a daily tracker. An answer updates exactly one source-linked record. It must never retroactively reinterpret an unlogged day as symptom-free.

### E. Living Research Receipt — tell the person what changed and why

When a personal source is edited, a citation is corrected/retracted, or a reviewed guideline changes, the dossier produces a short receipt: **old reading → new reading → exact changed source → whether the next action changes**. Keep “new article exists” distinct from “evidence conclusion changed.” If nothing material changes, stay quiet. A saved decision and later user-reported outcome/visit note close the loop, with patient words kept separate from clinician-documented findings.

**Why this matters:** continued subscription value can come from a maintained research record. The return trigger is a meaningful change, not a streak, push notification quota, or feed of abstracts.

## 5. First-use and deep-use contract

**First 60 seconds:** four textured intent cards in the existing warm porcelain/rose Clinical palette → one relevant question prompt → one short dossier summary: “what your records say,” “what remains open,” and one action. No intake questionnaire, diagnosis gate, research settings, medication list or required meal log. If there are no records, give a source-backed topic overview or a focused care question and say plainly that personal comparison is unavailable.

**Second layer:** one compact **Research Matrix**. The center is the user's question; clickable branches are **My records**, **Alternative explanations**, **Published research**, and **Decision/visit**. Use deliberate connectors with labels such as “explicit report,” “same-date context,” “published finding,” and “follow-up.” No unlabeled decorative chains. Open a branch to a source drawer with an exact record, a correction route and a short explanation. The clinical textured icon treatment is for navigation and meaning, not decoration.

**Deep layer:** a researcher can expand the full evidence table, study appraisal, source methods, counterexamples, change history and export. Default layout never exposes every dropdown at once. Mobile at 320px stacks the matrix; keyboard and screen-reader users get the same facts in logical order. One primary button per state.

**Example:** “Could last night's rajma explain morning bloating?” The dossier shows the recorded rajma meal and reported symptom, whether the symptom onset is timed, another meal and stress note as *context*, a tolerated rajma occasion if one exists, and a relevant general source with its population/limits. It says the records cannot isolate the cause. The next move might be to clarify onset or prepare a clinician question. No organ animation, fermentation peak or personal probability appears.

## 6. Architecture and source contract

Create a versioned, pure `GutResearchDossier` projection over the existing scoped stores. Suggested types: `GutResearchQuestion`, `GutGraphNode`, `GutGraphEdge`, `GutClaim`, `GutSourceSpan`, `GutApplicability`, `GutMissingFact`, `GutChangeReceiptV2`. Every personal node/edge has `ownerKey`, `profileId`, `sourceKind`, `sourceId`, `revision/update marker`, `occurredAt` or local date, `reportedAt`, time precision, and relation rule. Research nodes have PMID/DOI/URL, title, retrieval time, version/status, license/full-text availability, extracted span and reviewer state. **The graph is derived, not saved as a frozen causal verdict.** Persist only question, confirmed corrections, reviewed research claims, user decisions and last-reviewed fingerprints.

Relation enum: `explicitly_linked_report`, `reported_sequence`, `same_date_context`, `source_disagreement`, `general_research`, `decision`, `care_follow_through`. The rendering and counts depend on relation type. `same_date_context` can never increment explicit support. A scheduled medicine is never a taken dose. Diet reaction log time is never symptom onset. Deletion/tombstone or profile change removes or invalidates affected edges.

The retrieval pipeline is **retrieve → deduplicate → inspect status → classify question fit → extract source-bound fields → verify claims → human-review actionable wording → publish**. Start with Europe PMC/PubMed and a small curated guideline registry. Use a fixed generic query taxonomy; no raw user question, profile, meal name or timeline is transmitted to a public search service. A title/abstract-only result must be labeled as such. Recheck correction/retraction status before redisplaying a saved paper. Respect full-text license and show links rather than copying articles.

AI is optional and narrow: it may rank candidate studies, suggest which *source span* supports a claim, or route a question to a known topic. Code verifies IDs, source text, publication status, profile scope and all clinical rules. A model cannot invent a personal mechanism, diagnosis, dosage, confidence percentage or recommendation. Use a no-match/abstain path, evaluate against clinician-labeled examples, and fall back to deterministic copy. The [TypeSafe citation-check pattern](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/citation_check) illustrates a useful architecture: exact quote lookup first, then a bounded support/contradiction judgment; its demo thresholds are **not** medical validation. Keep all credentials server-side. Do not introduce an external AI service until privacy, cost and domain evaluation are approved.

## 8. Required test cases and research evaluation

Use synthetic fixtures for: one chai report, a tolerated chai counterexample, conflicting Diet/Gut reactions, same-day rajma and stress note, report saved two days after onset, no onset, altered recipe/portion, medication scheduled but never confirmed taken, profile switch, deleted source, a pediatric-only paper for an adult question, a corrected/retracted paper, research outage, no records, and an actual choice/visit return. Assert exact source navigation and all edge labels. Avoid tests that merely mirror UI text.

Before broad launch, run task sessions with users who have diagnosed IBS and users with gut symptoms without that diagnosis. Predeclare thresholds for: time to useful first answer; proportion who can correctly say what is *observed*, *general research* and *unknown*; source inspection success; whether a decision/visit became easier; logging burden; food fear and unintended restriction; accessibility. Ask about willingness to pay directly. Clinical reviewers must review the copy and any future protocol. These gates cannot be replaced by green code tests.

## 9. What $20/month would have to earn

The paid benefit hypothesis is **a maintained personal research dossier**, not access to raw abstracts or an endless tracker: exact-source evidence, alternative explanations, study applicability, one useful next fact, meaningful change receipts and a concise care brief. The free/paid boundary should be tested with users; do not paywall concerning-symptom guidance, a person's own record or basic correction. If users cannot name a concrete decision or care conversation the Studio improved, simplify or reposition rather than adding more cards.
